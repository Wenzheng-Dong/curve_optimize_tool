# Curve-family audit — what published robust-control curves actually deliver

This is a health check on **fourteen** curve families used as starting points
(*ansätze*) for noise-robust single-qubit gate design. For each one it measures
three things and shows what happens to them when the curve is forced into the
control basis this project optimizes in:

1. the **gate** it actually implements,
2. its **closure** — the first-order dephasing-cancellation condition,
3. its **projected area** — the second-order condition.

The third is the point of the exercise. The geometric-control literature and the
upstream toolbox both stop at closure; the second-order condition has no API
there, so no published family has been checked against it. Here it is measured
for all fourteen.

**All numbers live in [`TABLE.md`](TABLE.md) and [`audit.csv`](audit.csv), both
generated by [`make_report.py`](make_report.py).** Nothing in this README is
transcribed by hand, and the figures are rendered from the CSV rather than from a
second computation. The prose below quotes a few *rounded orders of magnitude*
(“of order 1e-4”) to be readable; every authoritative value is in the generated
table, and nothing here should be quoted as a measurement.

## Background in one paragraph

A single-qubit gate driven by `H_c = (Omega_x sigma_x + Omega_y sigma_y) / 2`
under quasi-static dephasing `epsilon sigma_z` maps onto a space curve: the
tangent direction is the error operator in the control frame, the curvature is
the drive amplitude, and arc length is time. First-order noise cancellation is
then the statement that the curve **closes**, `r(T) = 0`, and second order is
that its **projected area vanishes**, `int r x r_dot dt = 0`. Designing a robust
pulse becomes designing a closed, zero-area curve whose endpoint frame gives the
target gate. This project expands the two control fields in a sine series on
`[0, T]` and optimizes the series coefficients with the gate, closure and
zero-area conditions imposed as *hard equality constraints*.

## What is being compared

Every family arrives as samples — either an analytic curvature profile or a space
curve run through the inverse map — and is then **projected onto twelve sine
harmonics by plain least squares, with no constraint repair of any kind**. That
projection is the entry point of the optimizer, so the audit reports each
quantity at three stages:

| stage | meaning |
|---|---|
| `source` | the family as published, before anything is done to it |
| `projected` | after least squares onto the sine basis — the canonical protocol |
| `gate-fixed` | additionally shifted onto the gate hyperplane at minimum norm |

The `gate-fixed` stage is a **different protocol** and is reported only as a
diagnostic. It answers a question the literature treats as free: the gate error of
a published curve can always be removed exactly, by a minimum-norm shift along a
single direction in coefficient space. What does that cost?

## What the audit shows

**The gate correction is not free.** For the reference lemniscate family, removing
a gate error of order `1e-4` degrades closure by more than an order of magnitude.
The three conditions are coupled: they are not independent boxes to tick, and a
curve advertised as satisfying two of them will generally stop satisfying them
once the third is enforced. Figure `fig1_gate_fix_cost.png` shows all three stages
side by side for every planar family.

**The registry's `zero_area` flag is not a measurement.** Two families flagged as
zero-area measure a non-zero projected area under this project's criterion, one of
them at the `1e-2` level. The wording matters: this is a measurement under a
stated criterion and normalization, not a claim that the upstream flag is wrong —
normalizations differ, and only the number, its definition and its grid are
reported here.

**Endpoint conflict does not predict projection error.** The sine basis forces the
drive to vanish at both ends of the pulse, so a family whose curvature is non-zero
there cannot be represented exactly. That endpoint conflict is what the
`exact / approx / forced` tiers describe — but it does not order the families by
how badly the projection distorts them. One family with a 1.4% endpoint conflict
has a 91% projection error (its curvature is simply too high-frequency for twelve
harmonics), while another with a 100% endpoint conflict projects to 18%. Two
different kinds of "cannot be represented" are at work: *endpoint*-limited and
*bandwidth*-limited. Figure `fig2_tier_vs_relrms.png` plots one against the other.

**A constant-curvature arc is the minimum-norm solution in disguise.** The
least-squares projection of a circular arc turns out to be exactly parallel, to
machine precision, to the single coefficient-space direction that sets the gate
angle — because a constant function and the gate functional have proportional
sine spectra. So once its gate is corrected, a circular arc *is* the
minimum-energy solution of the gate constraint alone. It carries no information
about robustness whatsoever, which is worth knowing before using it as a control
group.

**Several families implement a different gate than the target.** Their total
turning is not near any `2 pi`-branch of the target angle; the audit flags those
rows rather than quietly comparing them. Two of them are closed *and* zero-area to
`1e-9` while implementing essentially the identity — the exact complement of the
reference family, which nearly hits the gate but owes area. Inputs like that are
the useful raw material for studying how much the starting curve influences the
optimization, and they exist upstream already.

**Starting cost spans two orders of magnitude.** Figure `fig3_starting_energy.png`
places every on-branch family's pulse energy against two reference lines: the
analytic lower bound for the gate alone, and the converged optimum under all three
constraints. The bound must be read at each family's *realized* gate angle, not at
the target's — a projection that misses the gate is allowed to sit below the
target's bound without violating anything.

## Reproducing

```bash
conda env create -f ../../environment.yml     # once; creates the `curve` env
conda activate curve
PYTHONPATH=../../../curvecontroltoolbox/src python make_report.py
```

The upstream clone is needed for the published families and is only ever read.
Without it the built-in families are still audited and the rest are reported as
skipped. `--render-only` rebuilds the table and figures from `audit.csv` alone,
which is also the check that the rendering never recomputes anything.

Fixed configuration: twelve harmonics, pulse duration 1, quadrature grid 20000
points (midpoint rule), target gate X(pi). Every reported quantity is
scale-invariant, so the choice of duration does not enter.

## Caveats

* These are **proxy-layer** results. They concern the geometric robustness
  conditions in the quasi-static limit, not an infidelity from a full physical
  model. Third order and above is uncontrolled, and leakage is not modelled.
* Non-planar families have no post-projection closure or area yet: rebuilding the
  curve from two-component coefficients needs the SU(2) propagator, which is the
  next stage of this project. Those cells read `n/a` rather than `0`.
* The `gate-fixed` columns are a diagnostic, not a recommended protocol. Runs that
  feed the project's own results always start from the unrepaired least-squares
  projection.
